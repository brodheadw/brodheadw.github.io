Is there a dark-matter neutrino signal from Boötes III?

In 2026 Ji & Wang reported an excess of high-energy neutrinos from Boötes III, a dwarf galaxy being torn apart by the Milky Way, and read it as possible dark matter annihilating into neutrinos (arXiv:2606.01853, Phys. Rev. D). We rebuilt their analysis from scratch on the same 1.13 million public IceCube events, then asked how often pure chance does as well.

How it was done

  • Data: IceCube's public track sample, April 2008 – July 2018 (1,134,450 events, 9.8 years of livetime), split into the paper's five detector configurations, each with its own effective area and smearing matrix.
  • Likelihood: the standard unbinned point-source likelihood used in the paper: Gaussian spatial term from each event's angular error, signal energy term from spectrum × effective area × smearing, background built from the data in sin(dec) × log₁₀E bins of 0.02 × 0.1.
  • Dark-matter model: χχ → νν̄, scanned over 100 masses from 1 TeV to 1 PeV. We model the spectrum as a line at the particle mass; the paper adds an electroweak continuum (HDMSpectra). That is why our best-fit mass is lower.
  • Check: the same code on NGC 1068, IceCube's best-known neutrino source, recovers the published fit.
  • Significance: right ascensions scrambled at random, the full mass scan repeated in every scramble; the 3-target trials factor follows the paper.

What this does and doesn't show

  • The excess is in the public data and our independent code finds it at the same strength.
  • About 3σ is a hint, not a discovery (5σ). IceCube's public data has produced several 3σ spots that later faded.
  • The trials factor counts the three stream cores the authors tested, not every target anyone has looked at, so the real look-elsewhere penalty is larger.
  • IceCube itself has not reported this source; its newer data (2018 onward) is not public yet and is the real test.

Code: bootes_repro.py (likelihood + trials) and export_web.py run on a Mac Studio; results published as bootes_analysis.json.
